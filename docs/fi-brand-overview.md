# FÍ Brand Overview — Ferðafélag Íslands

Source: fi.is (scraped 2026-03-26 via discover-brand skill)

## Identity

- **Founded:** 1927 (turning 100 in 2027)
- **Type:** Non-profit membership association (~9,000 members)
- **HQ:** Mörkinni 6, 108 Reykjavík
- **Tagline:** "Skráðu þig inn — drífðu þig út" (Sign in — get yourself out)

## Mission

To encourage travel around Iceland, awaken interest in the country's nature and history, and strengthen awareness of care in the relationship between people and nature.

Four pillars: trip programme, mountain hut network (41 huts), publishing, community life.

## Tone of Voice

**Warm institutional.** Not corporate, not casual. Like a trusted community elder who genuinely loves the outdoors — authoritative but never stiff, earnest without being preachy.

- **Earnest and heartfelt** — genuine passion about the land, no irony
- **Community-centred** — "we" is the default subject, volunteers thanked by name
- **Matter-of-fact on logistics** — dates, facts, instructions, no marketing fluff
- **Occasionally poetic** — reserved for moments of reflection, never forced
- **Icelandic-first** — all primary content in Icelandic, English is minimal/functional

## Key Values

1. **Stewardship** — "Með landið að láni." Custodian, not tour operator.
2. **Volunteerism** — the backbone of the organization
3. **Lýðheilsa** — outdoor activity as public health (Nordic friluftsliv)
4. **Education** — deepening understanding, not just getting people outside
5. **Inclusivity** — children's association, young people's association, all ages
6. **Conservation** — embedded in the mission, not campaigned

## What FÍ Is Not

- Not a commercial tour operator
- Not a wilderness adventure brand (no adrenaline marketing)
- Not an environmentalist advocacy group
- Not a modern lifestyle brand

## Archetype

If FÍ were a person: a knowledgeable, fit, grey-haired Icelander in well-worn hiking boots who knows every valley by name, volunteers on weekends to maintain trail markers, and genuinely believes getting people outside makes the nation healthier.
